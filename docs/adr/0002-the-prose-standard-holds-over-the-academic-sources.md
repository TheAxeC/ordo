# 0002. The prose standard holds over the academic sources

Status: proposed

## Context

The writing base is rebuilt from research-hub's `academic_writing_style.md`, `writing_judgment_framework.md` and `writing_quality_check.md` (`docs/academic-coverage.md`, the `rebuild: writing` rows). Where they differ from the prose standard (`skills/repo-setup/templates/docs/dev/prose-standard.md`), they allow up to three em dashes per paper (`writing_quality_check.md:60`) where the prose standard allows none (`:13`), keep a sentence that lays out the sections of an introduction (`writing_quality_check.md:103`) where the prose standard never describes what the page is about to do (`:41`), make the passive common in methods (`academic_writing_style.md:56`) where the prose standard rewrites it unless the actor is irrelevant (`:63`), and accept paragraphs of ten sentences (`writing_quality_check.md:117`) where the prose standard keeps them under roughly four (`:48`). None of the user's manuscript sources in research-hub has the phrasings "this paper is organized as follows", "the rest of this paper is organized" or "the remainder of this paper".

## Decision

The prose standard holds for every text the writing skills write or review. The academic rules add only what the prose standard lacks: terms defined at first use, clear antecedents, the hedging scale, tense per section, the clarity test, the reader's four questions, the "so what" per section, and the other rules the entry's rulings carry. No rule that contradicts the prose standard is carried.

## Alternatives rejected

- The prose standard with three exceptions for manuscripts and grants (the passive in methods, a sentence laying out the sections, paragraph length set by the argument): two rule sets to keep apart, and the passive exception is already the prose standard's "unless the actor is irrelevant".
- The academic sources' limits as written: they allow em dashes, which the user's rules ban, and they keep contradictions between the two rule sets.

## Consequences

One set of rules governs every text the user writes. A long introduction paragraph is reported, and the user judges it. A later writing skill that needs a rule the prose standard contradicts reopens this record with a superseding one.
