### Initial impression
The argument is clear and the contribution is stated: a survival network whose ECG evidence passes through block-term frequency and lead factors, with a separate linear clinical term, gives the highest one-year AUROC of the methods compared on ICD-coded MIMIC-IV labels, and its ECG term matches a compact CNN. The results are reported with care and every number agrees with the study's saved gold-label results, but six cited claims misstate or go beyond their sources, the paper never shows a reading of the factors it calls readable, and the calibration and positive predictive value statements rest on choices the text does not disclose. Impression score: 6/10.

### Strengths
1. The outcome definition is argued from evidence and applied the same way to every method. 4.1: "Hospital diagnoses were preferred to labels read from the ECG, since only 65.4% of 700 AF-labelled ECGs in MIMIC-IV showed AF on expert review", with a 30-day blanking window and AF and flutter counted together for a stated reason (Rix). 1.3: "Differences between them are therefore differences of model, and not of cohort or outcome definition."
2. The comparison with the clinical model is reported without overclaiming. 5.2: "It overlapped the interval of the clinical model, 0.7597 [0.7488, 0.7713], and the study reports no paired interval for this difference. The two estimates are therefore reported side by side." 6.1 and 7 keep the same reading ("not yet an established difference").
3. The occlusion readout corrects its own main bias and states its choices. 5.4: "The QRS complex has the highest amplitude of the beat, so zeroing it removes the most energy and changes the spectrogram most. Per unit of energy removed, the profile was highest in the PR window"; 4.3: "the zero replacement and the 25 ms window are choices of this readout."
4. The cohort table reports overlapping exclusions honestly. Table 1 caption: "Each exclusion count is stated against the 151,941 eligible patients, since the exclusion categories overlap", with control follow-up and later-ECG counts.
5. The Discussion reports evidence against its own reading. 6.2: "In a Swedish cohort of 70-year-olds, the PR interval measured in lead V1 was not related to incident AF, while a short P-wave duration was", and it limits the claimed agreement to "the location within the cardiac cycle only".
6. The Method is reproducible at the level of its settings: the spectrogram window and hop, K = 12 blocks with ranks 3 and 2, kernel lengths 3, 7 and 15, head sizes, optimiser, schedule, early stopping and the three-seed ensemble (3.1, 3.2), each of which agrees with the analysis as carried out.
