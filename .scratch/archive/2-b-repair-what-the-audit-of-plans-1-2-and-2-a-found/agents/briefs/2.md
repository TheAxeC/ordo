## What to build

In `skills/plan-orchestration/SKILL.md`:

1. **The loop and a stop.** Steps 10 says the loop ends only at a pause or when nothing unblocked is left; a stop blocks its step, and the loop moves on to the next unblocked step, as Steps 3 says.
2. **The builder and the ledger.** Steps 4 says the builder writes only its report, at the path the brief names in the worktree's copy of the ledger, and nothing else in the ledger; Steps 6 says the orchestrator saves that report into the main ledger.
3. **One meaning for `report`.** The dispatch block's `report` is the builder's report (the file the brief names). A shell launch's `--report` output (the `claude -p` JSON or the `codex -o` final message) goes under a field of its own, `output`, and Steps 6 says the orchestrator takes the builder's report from it into the `report` path. "Launching a builder" item 1's field list names `output` in place of `report`.
4. **`reviewer_report`.** Steps 7 records the review's path and usage in the dispatch block under `reviewer_report`.
5. **The round cap as a rule of its own** (ruling G). Steps 8 keeps the mechanics; the cap becomes one rule, stated once: at most `repair_rounds` rounds plus the one exception (a verification command left red or an acceptance item of the brief unbuilt, too large to fix at landing); the user's yes does not extend it; after the last round the step lands with its small fixes and everything else is booked as its own step. The "Not sent back" bullet names the finding by its text (a finding that changes the scope, a requirement, a public shape or an established decision), not by a row number.
6. **Models** (the user's ruling). "The two tiers, and the harnesses" says: the orchestrator and every agent (builder, reviewer) run on Claude Opus by default; the orchestrator may also run on Claude Fable, GPT Astra or GPT Sol; a builder or a reviewer may also run on GPT Sol; a builder or a reviewer never runs on Fable or Astra. The Rules line on vendor names says the skill carries no project name, and that the models it names are the options the user ruled.
7. **`inline` stays an optional executor**, chosen by the invocation or the configuration block; `agent` is the default. Keep Steps 4's `inline` bullet; say `agent` is the default where the choice is described.
8. **Skills invoked, never followed from memory.** A rule, and a line in "Resuming, and handing the plan over": `/spec`, `/refute` and `/land` are invoked through the runner every time, after a compaction too, never carried out from remembered text.
9. **Stops.** The Stops table gains two rows: a finding that changes the scope, a requirement, a public shape or an established decision (from Steps 8 and Anti-patterns row 2), and the closing step's roadmap diff (ruling 3a: `/roadmap done` shows its diff and the user approves it). The stop message is plain text in the report, an open item with its options, their pros and cons and one recommendation, and never goes through a question-box or multiple-choice tool.
10. **A rule that keeps being broken** (ruling 3b). "The recurring-findings pass" and Anti-patterns row 6 say: what is proposed is a check; a sharper sentence for the rule is proposed only when no command can check it.
11. **Anti-patterns.** Row 3 says "the repair rounds", not "the builder's one repair round". A new row: offering the user another repair round beyond the cap, why it fails (the cap is a rule the user's yes does not extend; the extra round only moves work that belongs in a new step) and what to do instead (land with the small fixes, book the rest as steps).
12. **Reports.** Every report opens with a position line, then the open items verbatim: the roadmap entry with its title, the plan step being worked as "step n of m" with its name, and the next step. Example: "Roadmap entry 2.B (repair what the audit found). Plan step 3 of 19: pin.sh. Next: step 4, collect_findings.py."
13. **Launching a builder**, the text only (the script belongs to a later step): item 1 says every path given to `templates/launch.sh` is absolute; `--label` is `<entry>/<step>` (ruling 2g); item 3 says the `claude -p` transcript folder is named after the builder's `--cwd`.
