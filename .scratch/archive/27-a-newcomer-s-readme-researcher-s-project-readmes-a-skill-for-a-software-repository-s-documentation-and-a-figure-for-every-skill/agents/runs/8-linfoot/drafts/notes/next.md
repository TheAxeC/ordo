That is why six levers could not move it. Both arms in `code/results/headroom.md`, reproduced independently on the cluster and locally to the third decimal.

Closed: the cosine-basis input does not fail on a spike, and on the test grid it is not behind at all. Across 192 cells at 200 replicates the two representations are level, 0.0626 against 0.0627, with the coefficient arm ahead in 106 cells and a paired difference of -0.00090 with standard error 0.00037. On the corner-spiked families, Clayton and its rotation, Frank and its reversal and Gumbel, it is ahead by 0.0004; on the bounded ones behind by 0.0005; and its worst family is the generator's own draws, the family built from this very basis. The split is sample size: behind by 0.0014 at n of 100, ahead by 0.0005, 0.0014 and 0.0016 at 200, 500 and 1000. A coefficient is a sample mean and 144 of them are noisy at a hundred points, and training draws n log-uniformly over 50 to 2000, so the validation loss that rejected the arm read that weighting. The input is 144 numbers against 3072 and trains eight times faster. Details in `code/results/representation_grid.md` and `code/results/basis_test.md`.

Closed: the independence floor, after two routes and five trained arms. The estimator's bias at exact independence was about twice the published model's, 0.1111 against 0.0552 at n = 100 averaged over the four forward analytic families, and the whole of its per-family disadvantage on Gaussian sits in that one row: the gap runs +0.0340 at L = 0 and is gone by L = 0.99.

The first route was the output layer, on the theory that a sigmoid cannot read values near zero. It cannot cheaply: the sigmoid is 1 - exp(-softplus(x)), and to return 0.02 it must drive its pre-activation to -3.9 through a region where the gradient is 0.02. Putting a sharpness on that softplus leaves the map identical at beta = 1, moves that same output to -0.22 with seven times the gradient at beta = 8, and changes the upper end not at all. Three arms trained at the matched budget. It does nothing: 0.0804 pooled at independence for the sigmoid, 0.0881 at beta = 8, 0.0813 at beta = 20. The floor is not an output-map artefact. The same run also retired the trade the paper had assumed between the two ends: no model returned a reading of 1 in any of 3200 cells at L = 0.99, and the sharpening improved that end rather than costing it, 0.0076 to 0.0037. Only the linear clipped variant has the saturation problem. Files: `code/results/output_ends.md`, `floor_beta` in `code/core/v2_model.py`.

The second route was the label prior, which is where the bias actually lives: at a boundary every error is positive, so the best any estimator can do is return the mean of what the sample leaves possible, and that mean is a property of the training distribution rather than the network. Four priors, selected by `LINFOOT_LABEL_PRIOR` in `code/core/phase2_gen.py`. The aggressive one, which draws exact independence a tenth of the time and concentrates the rest of the bottom band by squaring a uniform draw, lowers the bias by 40 to 45 percent (`code/results/shaped_ends.md:9-12`), to parity with the published model at the full training budget: 0.0639, 0.0461, 0.0288, 0.0202 against her 0.0552, 0.0432, 0.0277, 0.0208, and ahead at n = 1000. Overall error is unchanged, 0.0623 against 0.0620.

**It still must not be adopted, and rebuilding both benchmark tables is what shows why.** Scored on the grids the paper reports, it loses on six families of eight and loses worst on the generator row, 0.0738 to 0.0806 on the original grid and 0.0768 to 0.0835 on the wider one. The per-family figure pools six dependence levels of which independence is one, so a gain confined to that row cannot pay for losses spread across the other five. A third prior that also reinforces the 0.15 to 0.45 band recovers exactly that band, 0.0770 and 0.0675 against 0.0787 and 0.0713, and gives up 0.60 and 0.80 instead, 0.0672 and 0.0419 against 0.0521 and 0.0348. Every arm lands within 0.001 of 0.062 overall.

So the prior route is a zero-sum reallocation: mass moved to one band comes out of another and the total does not move. That is what the headroom result predicts, since an estimator already within 5 to 8 percent of the floor its own uncertainty model implies has nothing left to gain by reshaping the prior. **The shipped model keeps the default prior. Do not re-open this without a new idea; four arms have been run.** Rebuilt tables in `code/results/phase2_lowmass_grid.md` and `phase2_lowmass_wide.md`, the level breakdowns in `code/results/prior_ends.md` and `shaped_ends.md`.

Closed: the multiresolution cosine input closes the gap the single truncation left. Coefficients at three truncations, 4, 8 and 12, one channel each, every coefficient shrunk by its own estimated sampling noise, which is the coefficient counterpart of handing the network three histogram resolutions. Matched on the same copulas, the same 168000 examples and the same 60 epochs: single truncation 0.0696, three truncations 0.0678, histogram control 0.0675. That closes 0.0018 of the 0.0021 gap, so the Fourier input is level with the histogram on validation loss as well as on the test grid, at a seventh of the input size. Exact invariance survives it: reflecting either axis, swapping them, or taking logs of both moves the estimate by 0.00e+00. This matters beyond the bivariate case, because coefficients truncated by total degree grow polynomially where a grid grows as G^d, so it is the only one of the two representations that could go past two dimensions at all.
