- D2. Target formats. Options: `markdown` only, or `markdown` with one or more of `docx`, `pdf` and `latex`. Taken: `markdown` only. No Format profile question is asked, since that row exists only for a DOCX, PDF or LaTeX target.
- D3. Citation style. Options: APA 7th, Chicago 17th, MLA 9th, IEEE, Vancouver. Taken: IEEE. Reason: the default of computer science, the discipline row that holds machine learning. Vancouver would suit a medical imaging journal, and no venue is known. Cheaper option: none; the options do not differ in cost.
- D4. Length. Options: 9,000 words, or the length of the related-work section of the paper the review serves. Taken: 9,000 words. Reason: the page's recommendation, about 1,100 words for each of the eight strands; the paper the review serves is not named, so its length is not known. Cheaper option: the shorter length.
- D5. Review design. Options: a structured narrative review with a documented search, a PRISMA systematic review with a registered protocol, or a PRISMA-ScR scoping review. Taken: the structured narrative review with a documented search, with inclusion criteria (a) to (d) and exclusion criteria (e) to (i) of the row. Reason: the strands span eight method communities with no shared outcome measure a systematic review could pool, and the documented search still gives the databases, the query strings, the criteria and the counts. Criterion (a) carries the user's "methods admitted from any application domain". Cheaper option: the structured narrative review, the option taken; the PRISMA systematic review needs a registered protocol before the search.
- D6. Inclusion window. Options: foundational works from any year with a search over 2013 to 2026; the same with a search over 2018 to 2026; no year limit. Taken: foundational works from any year with a search over 2013 to 2026. Reason (a judgment): the partial least squares, tensor decomposition and optimal transport strands rest on definitions older than the search period, while entropic regularization became a learning tool in 2013 and the Gromov-Wasserstein, barycenter, dictionary and set-encoder work falls after it. Cheaper option: 2018 to 2026, which misses the first years of entropic and Gromov-Wasserstein learning work.
- D7. Output role. Options: the related-work basis of a later paper, named; a chapter of a later work, named; a standalone review. Taken: the related-work basis of a later paper. Reason: the Topic names the pipelines "such methods avoid or build on", which reads as the related work of a method. The paper's name is the user's to give and stays [GAP]. Cheaper option: none; the options do not differ in cost.

Reason: much of the transport and set-encoder work appears first or only on arXiv and in proceedings; preprints are flagged. Cheaper option: the list without preprints, which searches less.

- D11 (session, the instructions, the literature search). Options: one search agent over all eight strands; several search agents by strand group with a merge. Taken: four search agents by strand group, run at once, then one merge start. Group A: multilinear and tensor partial least squares, tensor regression, and tensor decompositions with an unaligned mode. Group B: entropic optimal transport, Gromov-Wasserstein and fused Gromov-Wasserstein distances, transport barycenters and dictionaries with learnable templates. Reason: the eight strands come from separate method communities, and one search agent would reach the saturation rules of its page on some strands while leaving others thin; a merge start then builds one matrix and one synthesis over the whole set. Cheaper option: one search agent, which searches less per strand.
