## Central thesis

This paper argues that a block-term tensor network (BTTN), in which every piece of ECG evidence passes through K = 12 learned frequency-by-lead block projections that can be read off the model, and to which age, sex and seven ECG machine measurements are added as a separate linear term, predicts one-year incident AF from one sinus-rhythm 12-lead ECG on ICD-coded MIMIC-IV outcomes with the highest point AUROC of seven models compared on the same cohort, folds and tensor; that its ECG-only form reaches discrimination comparable to a CNN on the same tensor; that one survival-trained fused model gives a higher point AUROC than a clinical Cox model at every horizon from 90 days to 5 years, with a risk gradient usable for targeted monitoring; and that the readability of its signatures is a structural property of the architecture that the paper submits to five faithfulness tests (results held at [GAP-F1] to [GAP-F5]).

Evidence types: AUROC point estimates with 95% patient-bootstrap CIs on gold ICD labels (E10 to E16, E20 to E35), calibration and operating-point values (E36 to E41), cohort accounting (E1 to E9e), design facts read from `code/core/bttn.py`, and the verified literature in `paper/phase1/annotated_bibliography.md`.

Argumentation pattern: engineering (Problem, Solution, Validation), as fits a method paper in a clinical AI journal, with the clinical validation carried by TRIPOD+AI-style reporting (collins2024).

## Sub-argument tree

- Central thesis
  - SA1: Incident-AF prediction from the sinus ECG needs a model whose ECG evidence is readable and whose readability is tested (Introduction, Related work)
    - AI-ECG predicts incident AF and complements clinical scores (raghunath2021, khurshid2022, christopoulos2020, jabbour2024)
    - Those models are black boxes with post-hoc or no explanations (hughes2021, storas2023, matsuo2026; research_gap.md gap 1)
    - Post-hoc attribution can be unfaithful and interpretable layers can leak (adebayo2018, hooker2019, mahinpei2021, havasi2022)
    - No block-term input factorisation is trained end to end with a survival objective for a clinical outcome (ye2020, vandenkerchove2025; research_gap.md gap 2)
  - SA2: BTTN restricts ECG evidence to readable block projections and adds clinical factors without entering that path (Methods; design facts)
  - SA3: The fused BTTN has the highest point AUROC at one year, and ECG-only BTTN is comparable to the CNN (Results 4.2; E10 to E16)
  - SA4: One survival BTTN + clinical model gives higher point AUROC than a clinical Cox model at every horizon, with calibration and a risk gradient usable for monitoring (Results 4.3, 4.4; E20 to E41)
  - SA5: The faithfulness of the signatures is assessed by five analyses (Methods 3.8; Results 4.5 held at GAP-F1 to F5)
  - Synthesis: SA2 gives the interpretability by structure, SA3 and SA4 show that this structure, with the clinical term, discriminates at the level of or above the comparators on gold labels, and SA5 is the measurement the literature in SA1 asks for.

## Synthesis

SA1 establishes the need for a readable and measured AI-ECG risk model. SA2 shows how BTTN makes the frequency and lead content of its ECG evidence readable and keeps clinical information on a separate readable term. SA3 and SA4 show, on gold ICD labels and as point estimates with CIs, that this design with the clinical term has the highest one-year point AUROC of seven models, that its ECG-only form is comparable to a CNN, and that one survival model gives higher point AUROC than a clinical Cox model at every horizon with a risk gradient usable for monitoring. SA5 is the measurement that SA1 says is required; its results fill the second half of the thesis when [GAP-F1 to F5] are filled.
