# Intended claims of the first draft

1. Atrial fibrillation is associated with a near fivefold excess of stroke, and its recorded incidence and prevalence rose over 50 years in Framingham. | evidence: empirical | sources: Wolf_1991, Schnabel_2015
2. The 2024 ESC guideline states that the clinical performance and broad applicability of AI solutions for AF are not yet known. | evidence: normative | sources: Van_Gelder_2024
3. Deep networks applied to the sinus-rhythm 12-lead ECG predict new-onset AF, and ECG models and clinical risk factors carry complementary information. | evidence: empirical | sources: Raghunath_2021, Jabbour_2024, Sau_2026, Zeidaabadi_2026, Habineza_2023, Riek_2026, Khurshid_2022, Christopoulos_2020, Khurshid_2025, Biton_2021
4. Reported discrimination depends on the outcome window and on label quality, and the identification of prior or concurrent AF is a different task from the prediction of dated incident AF. | evidence: empirical | sources: Tsiartas_2025, Attia_2019, Yuan_2023, Saiz_Vivo_2025, McKeen_2025
5. The explanations of published incident-AF models are post-hoc saliency or feature importance, and post-hoc attribution on the ECG is unstable and only partly tied to model weights. | evidence: empirical | sources: Khurshid_2022, Zeidaabadi_2026, Riek_2026, Arends_2026, arxiv_1810_03292, arxiv_1806_10758, Wagner_2024, Kashou_2021, Saiz_Vivo_2025
6. Models interpretable by design are preferable for high-stakes decisions, while one ECG study reports an accuracy cost of intrinsic interpretability and other designs report none. | evidence: normative and empirical | sources: Rudin_2019, Patlatzoglou_2025, arxiv_2007_04612, arxiv_2106_13314, arxiv_2205_10268, arxiv_2507_16761, arxiv_2504_02667
7. No published model places readable block-term frequency and lead factors on the predictive path of a survival network for incident AF. | evidence: definitional | sources: Ye_2020, Goulart_2020, Faes_2024, arxiv_2511_04292, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22227432
8. In BTTN the per-block frequency and lead factors lie on the predictive path of the ECG term, and the linear clinical term is added to the log-risk without passing through them. | evidence: definitional | sources: De_Lathauwer_2008, Kolda_2009, Faes_2024, Cox_1972, Katzman_2018, the project's results
9. On ICD-coded labels in MIMIC-IV-ECG at one year, ECG-only BTTN had an AUROC of 0.7397 [0.7275, 0.7517], comparable to the CNN at 0.7350 [0.7226, 0.7469] and above linear BTTR at 0.5967 [0.5824, 0.6115]. | evidence: empirical | sources: the project's results
10. BTTN with the clinical term had the highest one-year AUROC of the methods compared, 0.7776 [0.7664, 0.7888], with an interval above the CNN's and overlapping the clinical model's 0.7597 [0.7488, 0.7713]. | evidence: empirical | sources: the project's results
11. In the one-year design both ECG-only models had lower AUROCs than the clinical model. | evidence: empirical | sources: the project's results
12. One survival model gave the fused model AUROCs of 0.723 to 0.745 at 90 days, 1, 2 and 5 years, with point estimates above the clinical Cox model's at each horizon. | evidence: empirical | sources: the project's results
13. After cross-fitted recalibration the fused model's predicted one-year risks agreed with the observed rates across deciles (0.55 percent observed in the lowest decile, 11.4 percent in the highest, one inversion), with a Brier score of 0.039. | evidence: empirical | sources: the project's results
14. At each flagged fraction of 5, 10, 20 and 30 percent, the fused model had higher sensitivity and higher positive predictive value at the cohort incidence than the clinical model. | evidence: empirical | sources: the project's results
15. Per unit of signal energy removed, the occlusion response of the ECG term was highest in the PR window and lowest in the QRS window, while raw occlusion was dominated by the QRS window. | evidence: empirical | sources: the project's results, Zeiler_2014, Ivanovs_2021
16. The location of the PR-window response is in line with saliency reports of other incident-AF models and with cohort studies that tie the PR interval and P-wave indices in sinus rhythm to later AF, while one cohort found no PR-interval association. | evidence: empirical | sources: Khurshid_2022, Zeidaabadi_2026, Schnabel_2009, Cheng_2009, Magnani_2013, Tanaka_2021, Yogeswaran_2025, Magnani_2015, Emmanuela_2026, Ostrowska_2022
17. One reading of the fused model's gain is that the ECG term carries atrial-conduction information the clinical term does not measure, in line with the concept of atrial cardiomyopathy, offered as a hypothesis. | evidence: theoretical | sources: Goette_2016, Maezono_2025
18. ICD-coded AF diagnoses are mostly correct but incomplete, so some controls may have AF diagnosed outside hospital. | evidence: empirical | sources: Yao_2019, Malmo_2016, Rix_2012
19. AF and flutter codes are counted together because a coded flutter diagnosis did not reliably separate flutter from AF. | evidence: empirical | sources: Rix_2012
20. Hospital diagnoses are a better outcome source than machine-read ECG labels in MIMIC-IV, where a third of AF-labelled ECGs did not show AF on expert review. | evidence: empirical | sources: Valasakis_2026, Johnson_2023, https://doi.org/10.13026/4nqg-sb35
21. An occlusion profile depends on the replacement value and on the size of the occluded window. | evidence: empirical | sources: arxiv_1702_04595, _imi__2025
22. Readable factors do not by themselves show that a nonlinear head routes its decision through them. | evidence: theoretical | sources: arxiv_2106_13314, arxiv_2007_04612
23. Published incident-AF results on the same source hospital and on MIMIC-IV differ from this study in design, horizon and label source, so they are context and not a ranking. | evidence: empirical | sources: Sau_2026, Zeidaabadi_2026, Jabbour_2024, Raghunath_2021, Riek_2026, Tsiartas_2025
24. The fused model's operating point at the top 10 percent of risk lies in the range of one externally validated model's operating point in a different population. | evidence: empirical | sources: Pfeifer_2026, the project's results
25. The clinical comparator uses a subset of the CHARGE-AF factors and omits the PR interval, a known AF predictor. | evidence: definitional | sources: Alonso_2013, Cheng_2009, Magnani_2013
26. Age in this study is the MIMIC-IV anchor age, which is not the age at the index ECG. | evidence: definitional | sources: Johnson_2023
