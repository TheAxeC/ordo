## Argument blueprint

### Central thesis

This paper argues that a block-term tensor network whose ECG evidence passes through readable frequency and lead factors, with age, sex and ECG intervals added as a separate linear term, predicts incident AF from one sinus-rhythm 12-lead ECG on ICD-coded MIMIC-IV labels with higher discrimination than a clinical risk-factor model and a CNN, because its ECG term alone reaches the CNN's discrimination where linear block-term regression does not, the fused model has the highest one-year AUROC and stays above the clinical model at four horizons read from one survival model, and it is calibrated and flags more future cases than the clinical model at every flagged fraction, based on cross-validated results on gold-label samples of 9,275 patients by design (1,855 cases with four controls each) and 20,000 patients. Per unit of signal energy, its ECG term responds most to the PR window of the beat, the part of the cardiac cycle that cohort studies of the PR interval tie to later AF.

The thesis is arguable: the fused model's interval overlaps the clinical model's at one year, the CNN baseline is compact, the clinical comparator omits the PR interval, and published models report higher values in other designs.

### Sub-arguments

#### Sub-argument 1: The ECG term of BTTN reaches the discrimination of a CNN under the same protocol, which linear block-term regression does not, while its frequency and lead factors stay readable.
- **Evidence**: `table1_cis.csv` (gold, `source_artifact` = `bench_spectral_icd_h365.npz`): ECG-only BTTN 0.7397 [0.7275, 0.7517]; CNN 0.7350 [0.7226, 0.7469]; linear BTTR 0.5967 [0.5824, 0.6115]. Literature: interpretable-by-design models are preferable in high-stakes use [@Rudin_2019]; one ECG study reports an accuracy cost of intrinsic interpretability [@Patlatzoglou_2025].
- **Reasoning**: Same cohort, folds, seeds and spectral tensor for all three methods, so the comparison isolates the model. Overlapping intervals of BTTN and the CNN support "comparable"; the non-overlapping interval of BTTR shows that the nonlinear survival generalisation is what reaches CNN-level discrimination. The readable factors are a property of the architecture (Method 3.1, 3.3; figure `bttr-to-bttn`).
- **Counter-argument**: The CNN is a compact three-layer network, so parity with it says little about parity with the large networks of published work [@Raghunath_2021]; and overlapping intervals are not a test of equivalence.
- **Rebuttal**: Concede and limit. The paper claims comparability under one protocol on one cohort, names the CNN's size, makes no equivalence or superiority claim, and does not compare its values with published models across designs [@Tsiartas_2025].

#### Sub-argument 2: Adding the clinical term to BTTN gives the highest discrimination of the methods compared at one year, and one survival model keeps the fused model above the clinical model at 90 days, 1, 2 and 5 years.
- **Evidence**: `table1_cis.csv`: fused 0.7776 [0.7664, 0.7888], clinical 0.7597 [0.7488, 0.7713], CNN 0.7350 [0.7226, 0.7469]. `multihorizon_icd_spectral.csv` (gold by name): fused 0.723 [0.701, 0.745], 0.727 [0.711, 0.743], 0.740 [0.727, 0.753], 0.745 [0.735, 0.755] against clinical Cox 0.710, 0.711, 0.727, 0.731 at 90 days (onset 31 to 120 days), 1, 2 and 5 years. Literature: ECG and clinical information are complementary [@Khurshid_2022; @Christopoulos_2020].
- **Reasoning**: The fused model contains the clinical model's factors and adds the ECG term; a higher AUROC in both designs and at every horizon indicates that the ECG term adds information the clinical factors do not hold. The interval of the fused model lies above the CNN's at one year.
- **Counter-argument**: The one-year gain over the clinical model is small, the intervals overlap, and no paired interval is in the committed files; the four horizons share one model and overlapping patients, so they are not four independent confirmations. The clinical factors are fewer than CHARGE-AF's [@Alonso_2013] and omit the PR interval, which cohort studies tie to AF [@Cheng_2009; @Magnani_2013], so the comparator is weaker than a full clinical score. The labels are hospital ICD codes, which missed 20 percent of AF cases in a pooled estimate [@Yao_2019] and 26.3 percent in a population study [@Malmo_2016].
- **Rebuttal**: Concede and limit, with acknowledgement as a limitation. The text reports estimates and intervals side by side, states the overlap, prints no difference as an effect estimate, and presents the horizons as the behaviour of one model. The narrower comparator and the incompleteness of coded AF are stated in the limitations with their sources; coded AF diagnoses are mostly correct (positive predictive value 88 to 92.6 percent [@Yao_2019; @Malmo_2016; @Rix_2012]), so the cases are mostly true AF.

#### Sub-argument 3: The fused model is calibrated after recalibration and flags more future AF cases than the clinical model at every flagged fraction.
- **Evidence**: `clin_eval_operating_points.csv` (gold scores, `clin_eval.py` line 21): Brier 0.039 fused, 0.0398 clinical; at the top 10 percent, sensitivity 0.272 against 0.240, PPV at the cohort incidence 0.1155 against 0.1019, number needed to monitor 8.7 against 9.8; sensitivity and PPV higher at 5, 20 and 30 percent too. `risk_strata.csv`: observed one-year AF 0.55 percent in the lowest decile and 11.4 percent in the highest, with mean predicted 0.61 and 12.3 percent. Literature: decision curves [@Vickers_2006]; an external operating point of sensitivity 31 percent and specificity 92 percent at 9.7 percent flagged [@Pfeifer_2026].
- **Reasoning**: Observed and predicted rates agree across deciles, and at fixed flagging rates the fused model finds more of the patients who develop AF, which is what a screening use needs.
- **Counter-argument**: The Brier difference is small; the decision curve separates the models only at low thresholds; the deciles have one inversion (4.95 against 4.15 percent); probabilities come from a recalibration on the evaluation sample with two-fold cross-fitting.
- **Rebuttal**: Acknowledge as a limitation. The text claims calibration of the fused model and higher sensitivity and PPV at the four flagged fractions, makes no claim that the Brier score is better, describes the decision curve as the figure shows it, and reports the inversion.

#### Sub-argument 4: Per unit of signal energy, the ECG term's response to beat-aligned occlusion is highest in the PR window and lowest in the QRS window, the part of the cycle that PR-interval cohort studies tie to later AF.
- **Evidence**: `beat_saliency_segments.csv` (gold by the default `--source icd` of `figures/beat_saliency.py`): energy-normalised PR window 0.00216, ST 0.00157, P 0.00152, T 0.00127, QRS 0.00086; raw QRS 0.0232 against PR 0.0037. Method: occlusion sensitivity [@Zeiler_2014; @Ivanovs_2021]. Literature: saliency in other incident-AF models pointed to the P-wave and PR interval [@Khurshid_2022; @Zeidaabadi_2026]; the PR interval predicted AF in Framingham (hazard ratio 1.12 per 20 ms; first-degree block 2.06) [@Schnabel_2009; @Cheng_2009], in older black and white adults [@Magnani_2013], in an older Japanese population (1.75 for first-degree block) [@Tanaka_2021] and in Black adults [@Yogeswaran_2025]; P-wave indices show the same direction [@Magnani_2015; @Emmanuela_2026].
- **Reasoning**: Zeroing a high-energy part of the beat perturbs the spectrogram most, so raw occlusion favours the QRS window; dividing by the energy removed gives the response per unit signal. The resulting profile places the ECG term's response in the part of the cycle that both published explanations and cohort studies of atrioventricular conduction single out.
- **Counter-argument**: The readout is a post-hoc perturbation explanation, which the paper's own Related work calls unreliable [@arxiv_1810_03292; @Arends_2026]; it is computed on 120 highest-risk training ECGs; its zero replacement and 25 ms window shape the map [@arxiv_1702_04595; @_imi__2025]; the PR window is a fixed offset from the R peak that also holds the end of the P wave at short PR intervals, so it is not the measured PR interval of the cohorts; P and ST are close, so the atrial specificity is limited to the PR window; one cohort found no relation of the PR interval in lead V1 to incident AF [@Ostrowska_2022]; and no measured test on gold labels shows that the block factors carry the decision [@arxiv_2106_13314].
- **Rebuttal**: Acknowledge as a limitation, and reframe. The paper presents the profile as a description of what the fitted ECG term responds to, names the windows by their offsets, states the replacement and window choices, claims agreement with the cohort literature in location within the cycle only, reports the contrary cohort beside the supporting ones, and offers the atrial reading as a hypothesis [@Goette_2016; @Maezono_2025]. The readability claim rests on the architecture, not on this readout, and the gold-label faithfulness test is named as future research.

### Logical flow

- Introduction: AF burden and the value of prediction; black-box models with post-hoc explanation; the missing readable survival model; research question; contributions.
- Related work: incident-AF AI-ECG and its task definitions; clinical models, ECG intervals as predictors and fusion; interpretability by design against post-hoc explanation; block-term models; gap confirmed.
- Method: spectral tensor; block-term layer with frequency and lead factors; BTTR beside BTTN (figure `bttr-to-bttn`); temporal convolution, pooling, head, Cox objective; separate clinical term; readability by construction.
- Experiments: ICD-coded cohort with 30-day blanking and AF and flutter counted together; two designs; baselines on the same factors and tensor; cross-validation; metrics; occlusion readout with its windows and choices.
- Results: cohort counts against the eligible total; one-year discrimination (supports sub-arguments 1 and 2); horizons from one survival model (supports sub-argument 2); calibration, deciles and operating points (supports sub-argument 3); occlusion profile (supports sub-argument 4).
- Discussion: results beside published models with design differences; complementarity of ECG and clinical information; readable ECG term with separate clinical term; the PR-window profile beside the PR-interval and P-wave cohorts, with the contrary cohort; counter-arguments of each sub-argument; limitations (label accuracy, anchor age, comparator without the PR interval, readout choices); future research.
- Conclusion: thesis restated within MIMIC-IV; validation outside one health system as the next requirement.

The pattern is the engineering one (problem, solution, validation), with the clinical-prediction reporting items of TRIPOD+AI [@Collins_2024].

- Sub-argument 2 carries the "more accurately than the clinical model" clause of the research question, and its evidence is a consistent direction without a paired interval. The weak-argument sign below applies. The comparator's omission of the PR interval adds to its counter-argument risk.
- Sub-argument 4 is the weakest: one descriptive readout on 120 training ECGs, with fixed windows and zero replacement. The cohort literature now supports the interpretation of its location, but not the readout itself, and one cohort runs against it. It supports the cardiac-cycle clause of the narrowed research question only, and the thesis puts it in a separate sentence for that reason.
- The faithfulness clause of the research question in `config.md` is not carried (planner decision 1): no gold-label result supports it (`outline.md`, "Structure check" and "Analyses left out", items 1 to 6).

### Weak-argument signs

- Sub-argument 2: a counter-argument at least as strong as the argument on one clause. The claim of higher discrimination than the clinical model rests on overlapping intervals with no paired test. One sign; noted, drafting continues, and the draft writer words the claim as estimates with overlapping intervals.
- Sub-argument 4: a hasty generalization if the profile is read beyond the fitted model, since it comes from the 120 highest-risk ECGs of the training sample. One sign; noted, drafting continues, and the draft writer keeps the profile a description of the model. The second candidate sign, a key term used inconsistently ("PR segment" of the readout against "PR interval" of the cohorts), is avoided by the fixed terms below.

No core argument has two or more signs.

### Notes for the draft writer

- **Fixed terms, each defined once and then repeated**: "BTTN" (the network); "block-term layer"; "frequency factor" and "lead factor" (A_k, C_k); "ECG term" (BTTN without clinical factors, called "ECG-only BTTN" in tables); "clinical term" (the linear risk-factor term); "fused model" (BTTN with the clinical term); "clinical model"; "CNN"; "BTTR"; "incident AF"; "one-year design" and "survival design"; "P window", "PR window", "QRS window", "ST window", "T window" for the occlusion readout, each defined once by its offsets from the R peak; "PR interval" and "P-wave duration" only for the measured intervals of the cited cohorts and of the clinical term. Do not rotate synonyms such as "glass-box", "architecture" or "network" for BTTN.
- **Words not used about this paper's results**: "faithful" or "faithfulness" as a finding, "causal", "significant" or "significantly", "outperforms" for the comparison with the clinical model, "drives" for the occlusion response, "substrate" as a result (it may appear once in 6.2 as the literature's concept, hedged), "detection" or "identification" for the task.
- **Numbers**: every number comes from the files in `outline.md`, "Project results", at the precision the file holds (four decimals in the one-year table, three in the horizon table). No difference between two AUROCs is printed as an effect. No faithfulness number. No number from `RESULTS.md`. Name the interval type: bootstrap mean with percentile interval in the one-year table, full-sample estimate with percentile interval in the horizon table. The one-year sample is stated as the design count: 1,855 cases with four controls each. The cohort table states each exclusion count against the 151,941 eligible patients.
- **Literature numbers**: a number from a cited study is taken from its annotation in `review.md` and given with the study's own measure (hazard ratio per 20 ms, per standard deviation, or for first-degree block; pooled sensitivity and positive predictive value).
- **Content kept out**: self-supervised pretraining (no mention in the method or results); external datasets and their results, and CODE-15 and PTB-XL in the data availability statement; any rising risk or rising saliency toward onset; the block-structure ablation, block-count sweep, probes and factor-removal curve; any statement that AF detection or identification is incident prediction; PTB-XL results of the cited technical report.
- **Honest statements required**: the ECG-only models are below the clinical model at one year in both designs (one plain sentence in 5.2, with the complementarity reading in 6.1); the fused model's interval overlaps the clinical model's; age is the MIMIC-IV anchor age; the clinical term omits the PR interval; onset is dated by admission and coded AF misses cases diagnosed outside hospital; the occlusion readout is on training ECGs, with zero replacement, 25 ms windows and fixed offsets from the R peak; one cohort found no PR-interval association [@Ostrowska_2022].
- **Hedging**: "comparable" for BTTN against the CNN; "highest AUROC of the methods compared" for the fused model; "consistent with" or "in line with" for agreement with the literature; "one reading is" for the atrial interpretation and for the reading that the ECG term carries atrial-conduction information the clinical term lacks.
- **Figure `bttr-to-bttn`**: the caption follows the caption requirements under "Planned figures" in `outline.md` (cite [@Faes_2024]; state the pooling of the reported model, attention and maximum along time; say that the clinical term is not drawn).
- **Manuscript prose standard**: no software wording about the project's own work (no file, script, job or pipeline names in the text); headings as labels; paragraphs of about 120 to 200 words on one idea; sentences under about 20 words where the content allows; no em dashes or dash asides; arrows only inside mathematics.
- **Model name**: state in 2.4 that BTTN differs from the block-term tensor neural networks of [@Ye_2020], which compress weights and do not read their factors.
