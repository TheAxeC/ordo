# the search stage: Research gap

Stated from the verified sources in `annotated_bibliography.md` (working index numbers in brackets) and from the searches recorded in `search_strategy.md`. These are findings about the literature, for the structure architect and argument builder; they are not manuscript prose and contain no result of this project.

## Gap statement

Deep-learning models predict incident AF from a single sinus-rhythm 12-lead ECG (AUROC 0.85 at 1 year in [2]; 0.705 to 0.823 at 5 years across sites in [3]; 0.78 internally and 0.77 in MIMIC-IV in [6]) and perform comparably to, and complement, clinical risk scores [3, 4, 6, 14]. Every verified incident-AF model of this kind is a black-box CNN or foundation model [1 to 3, 6, 7, 9, 10], and none of their abstracts reports an explanation analysis; where an ECG model's explanation is offered in the verified sources it is post hoc (LIME in [49], Grad-CAM in [50], SHAP in [12]) and none of these sources tests whether the explanation is faithful to the model, although post-hoc attributions are known to fail model and data randomisation checks [45], often do no better than random importance under removal and retraining [46], and gave physicians no usable information on ECGs [50]. Block-term tensor structure has been placed inside neural networks only to compress weights [34 to 36], and has been used as readable components only in unsupervised decompositions [31, 32], in supervised linear or two-stage pipelines [26 to 29, 33, 37], or in generic multilinear and alignment-based image networks without a clinical outcome or named physiological signatures [38, 39]. Concept-bottleneck work shows that an interpretable intermediate layer feeding a flexible predictor can leak information outside the readable representation [43, 44], so a model's interpretability has to be measured, which is also the standard its critics ask for [41, 47]. In the searches run, no verified study develops and evaluates incident-AF prediction on MIMIC-IV-ECG with ICD-10-dated outcomes against a clinical risk-factor model; MIMIC-IV appears only as an external test set [6, 9, 10] or for AF detection [11]. The gap this paper fills is an interpretable-by-design, survival-trained ECG model whose ECG evidence passes through readable frequency and lead block factors, compared on one gold-label MIMIC-IV-ECG cohort with a clinical risk-factor model and a CNN, with the faithfulness of its signatures tested rather than asserted.

## Gaps by category

1. Methodological gap: interpretability without measured faithfulness. AI-ECG AF studies either report no explanation analysis in their abstracts [1 to 3, 6, 7, 9, 10] (a full-text check would settle whether any includes one) or use post-hoc attribution without faithfulness testing [12, 49, 50]. The interpretability literature provides the tests (randomisation checks [45], removal-and-retrain [46], graded faithfulness [47], leakage analysis [43, 44]) but none of the verified AF sources applies them. Searches P2, P3 and WS6 found no ECG study with a quantitative faithfulness evaluation.

2. Methodological gap: block-term structure on the predictive path with a survival objective. Block terms in networks compress weights [34 to 36]; supervised block-term features feed a separate classifier [37]; linear block-term regression is the predecessor [27 to 29]; multilinear interpretable networks [38] and B-cos networks [39] are evaluated on images. No verified source trains a block-term input factorisation end to end with a nonlinear head and a Cox partial-likelihood objective [51 to 53] for a clinical outcome.

3. Comparator gap: interpretable ECG model versus both a clinical model and a black box on the same cohort. Prior work compares a CNN with CHARGE-AF and finds them complementary [3, 4, 6]; interpretable-feature AF prediction exists only at minutes-ahead horizons on small data [13] or with feature-based models whose explanations are post hoc [5, 12]. The comparison of an interpretable ECG model against both comparators with fusion of clinical factors is not reported in the verified sources.

4. Population and label gap: MIMIC-IV-ECG with ICD-10-dated incident outcomes. MIMIC-IV-ECG [19] linked to MIMIC-IV diagnoses [18] supports dated outcomes, yet the verified incident-AF studies use it only for external testing [6, 9, 10], and recent foundation models rely on labels extracted from ECG reports [10]. Search P1 (11 hits) found no development study on these data with ICD-10 dated labels.

5. Physiological-plausibility gap: readable signatures checked against known substrate markers. Sinus-rhythm markers of AF risk are established for P-wave duration [61] and P-wave terminal force in V1 [62, 63], and spectral measures of atrial fibrillatory rate are reviewed [59, 60], but deep AF models do not expose frequency-by-lead evidence that can be compared with these markers. Time-frequency ECG inputs have been used for detection of current arrhythmia [58], not for incident prediction (search P4). Numerical f-wave band values could not be verified from abstracts and remain unverified (`search_strategy.md`, Unverified).

## Coverage limits carried forward

- No verified ECG-specific quantitative faithfulness study was found; the faithfulness argument rests on general interpretability sources [43 to 48] plus one qualitative ECG study [50].
- Only one verified time-frequency deep-learning ECG source [58] passed screening.
- The 2020 ESC guideline [17] has no abstract in the records read; any statement drawn from it needs a full-text check by the citation agent.
- A Hasselt University master's thesis on block-term tensor regression for AF from multi-lead ECG appeared in search WS6 but could not be verified (HTTP 503) and is not used; if it is the project's predecessor work, the authors should supply its citation.
