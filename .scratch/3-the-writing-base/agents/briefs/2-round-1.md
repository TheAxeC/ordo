1. **Spec 1, the contrast window.** The window between "not" and the comma or "but" is 1 to 10 words, and a word of the window is letters, digits, `'` and `-` only, so any other punctuation (such as `)`, `;`, `:`) ends it and there is no match (`not done), a` is no contrast). A match is skipped when, in the same sentence before "not", one of if, when, unless, whether, because, although, since, while stands at the start of the clause that holds "not" (a subordinate clause, as in "If it is not found in the cache, the script reads it"). Cases: the concept-note forms (five words: "It is not a limitation to apologise for, it is the method."; nine words: "It is not an offer to do machine learning for your group, it is a request."), an eleven-word window (no match), the parenthesis form (no match), a subordinate "If ... not ..., the ..." (no match), and the same sentence without "If" (match).
2. **Spec 2, the report.** Under "Wrong or impossible in the brief", record that the brief's clean-file near miss "code with an em dash in a fence" cannot pass under its own `non-ascii` rule, and name where the case went (the forms file, lines 372 and 376). Record also that the brief's concept-note evidence claimed 3 contrasts where the brief's 1-to-4-word rule gives 2, and that ruling 1 changed the window.
3. **Spec 3, equal-length across sections.** A run of equal-length sentences does not cross a heading: a Markdown heading, and in LaTeX `\section`, `\subsection`, `\subsubsection` (starred and with an optional argument), `\begin{abstract}` and `\end{abstract}` end the run. A run still continues across a paragraph break and across the lines left out (lists, tables, code). Cases: the reviewer's `eq.md` and `eq.tex` probes (no flag), and the same five sentences split by a paragraph break only (flagged).
4. **Proof 1 to 4, rules and branches with no case.** Add a case that turns red under each revert the refuter report lists: a sentence ending at `!` and at `?`; a word joined across `'` and across `-` changing a word count; `tabular*`, `tabularx` and `longtable` rows as table rows; the closing fence character (a `~~~` line does not close a backtick fence); an escaped `\$` not opening math; CR line ends; a frontmatter opener with trailing spaces; the `(?!but\b)` guard of the contrast "but" form; the tie order of two flags on one line; the whitespace collapse over tabs and CR; the `\end{abstract}` line's text read as prose (see ruling 5).
5. **Behaviour 1, LaTeX text sharing a line with a structure command.** The text on a line after a heading's closing brace, before or after `\begin{...}` or `\end{...}` of abstract, a list or a table, and after `\item`, is read as prose of the kind of the place it stands in (the section's text, a list item, running prose after an `\end`). Only the command itself is left out; the heading's title stays the heading. Cases: each of the reviewer's five probes, flagged as the rules give.
6. **Behaviour 2, begin and end on one line.** The depth of tables, lists and left-out environments is tracked per command, in order along the line, so a `\begin{X}` and its `\end{X}` on one line leave the depth as it was. Cases: the reviewer's p2.tex and p4.tex probes, flagged as the three-line forms are.
7. **Behaviour 3, `\section[Short]{Title}`.** A LaTeX heading takes an optional `[...]` argument before its title, for all three levels and starred forms; the title in braces is the heading text. Case: `--limit 'Methods=1'` on `\section[Short]{Methods}` flags section-words at that line.
8. **Behaviour 4, indented fences.** A fence opens at any indentation of three or more backticks or tildes at the start of the line's text, and closes at a line of the same character, at least as many, at any indentation. Cases: a fence under a list item (the reviewer's p7.md, no flag inside), and `skills/repo-setup/SKILL.md` giving no flag at line 59.
9. **Behaviour 5, table cells.** Each Markdown table cell, and each LaTeX tabular cell (split at `&` and `\\`), is its own unit for sentence-based checks (contrast, equal-length, colon-lists): no sentence runs across a cell boundary. Case: a row "| a | not guess | The open item, booked |" (no contrast), and a single cell holding a contrast (counted).
10. **Behaviour 6, blockquotes.** The leading `>` markers of a Markdown blockquote line are stripped before any check, and the rest is read as the line it quotes (a list marker stays a list marker). Case: `> - a quoted list item` (no dash-aside), and `> a -- b` (flagged).
11. **The four items the reviewer left to the orchestrator.** Each is ruled here, inside the prose standard:

    - LaTeX code and references are removed like Markdown code spans: the argument of `\verb` (any delimiter), `\texttt`, `\url`, `\href` (first argument), `\label`, `\ref`, `\eqref`, `\cref`, `\autoref`, `\cite` and its variants (`\citep`, `\citet`, `\parencite`, `\textcite`), and the `lstinline` and `mintinline` forms. Cases: the reviewer's four forms (no flag), and the same text outside the command (flagged).
    - A one-line data row is left out of `semicolons`, as prose standard section B says, in both file forms: a paragraph of one line that ends without a sentence end (`.`, `!` or `?`), or a LaTeX line whose text is one command with its arguments (such as `\keyoutput{...}{...}`). Cases: a keywords line "Keywords: a; b; c; d" (not counted) and a one-line paragraph that is a sentence with semicolons (counted).
