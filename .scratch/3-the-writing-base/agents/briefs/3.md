## What to build

Each of the 52 findings of step 2 not marked right by both markers, and each failure of a gate condition that `2-report.md` names, has a cause in the text of `/writing`: a rule the review agent was not given, or a rule whose words let it read the passage wrongly. The step changes `skills/writing/SKILL.md`, `skills/writing/references/rules.md` and, where a fix belongs there, `skills/writing/references/registers.md`, so that the review agent, reading them, would not make any of those findings in that form. A fix is a rule the text states, never a reference to this draft, these findings or this plan. Each rule is stated once, in the one place `docs/dev/skill-layout.md`, "Where a rule goes", gives it, and follows the prose standard.

The fixes, by kind. Each kind lists the findings it covers, by the numbering of `2-marks.md` (manuscript findings 1 to 120 in the report's order, grant findings by the report's numbers). A rule of the prose standard is named by its section and never restated (ADR 0001); a fix adds only what the section lacks.

1. **A replacement ends the break and does not change what the passage claims.** The prompt's "A finding" states that a replacement drops no claim or clause of the passage, changes no claim's scope or strength, and adds no number; that a relation or a limit the replacement states holds in the draft's own data; that a number the replacement gives stands in the draft as the replacement uses it, and a number the agent would compute (a ratio, an average, a percentage) is given in square brackets as a fact for the author; that a replacement reads as a sentence in place of the passage; and that a replacement for a vague qualifier never cuts it so that a usual case becomes a universal one. A change of wording that another finding on the same passage proposes is carried in the replacement, as kind 6 says. Covers manuscript 2, 3, 4, 12, 15, 16, 17, 19, 20, 21, 23, 25, 29, 45, 46, 54, 56, 59, 68, 82, 84, 88, 94, 113 and grant 41, 45, 62.
2. **A finding stands only where the rule's words cover the passage.** The prompt states that a passage is reported under a rule only when the rule's own words describe the break, that a defect no rule's words describe is not reported, and that a use of a word the prose standard's section A keeps is not reported. In `references/rules.md`, each section the runs misapplied says, in its own words, what it does not cover:
   - "Evidence for claims": a claim whose evidence the draft gives, in its text, a table or a figure, cited or not, is not reported under it; a claim the draft's own data hold only in part is reported under "Limitations and alternative interpretations", with the limit as the replacement; a number or claim the draft's own data contradict is a question of content, which no rule of the review covers. Covers manuscript 1, 2, 3, 4, 13.
   - "Terms defined at first use": a technical term only; a term defined at first use whose value, selection rule or person is missing, and a sentence that states a criterion other than the one the author meant, are not reported under it. Covers manuscript 28, 29, 34 and grant 8, 9.
   - "Clear antecedents": a pronoun standing alone; a determiner with its noun ("this variation", "such summaries") is not reported, and the replacement names the noun, never another pronoun. Covers grant 41, 42, 43.
   - "Most specific term": a general word where a more specific term exists for the same thing; a word that is the right term for what it names, and a missing number or threshold, are not reported under it. Covers manuscript 52 and grant 33.
   - "Hedging": a hedge of the strength the evidence carries on an interpretation is not reported. Covers manuscript 21.
   - "Reader's question per section" and "Paragraph point": a first sentence that states the section's contribution or the paragraph's point is not reported for being general. Covers manuscript 49.
   - The prompt's sentence on section A covers manuscript 92 ("just" as a minimality claim).
3. **A term is defined where it first appears in the whole draft, and a term replaced is replaced at every use.** "Terms defined at first use" states that the finding names the term's first use in the whole draft (a `.tex` read as one document, a `.docx` from paragraph 1); that a replacement introduces no term before the place the draft defines it; and that a replacement that removes or renames a term gives a finding, or a place with its replacement, for every use of it. Covers grant 1, 4, 5, 12.
4. **"delete" only where the rule cuts.** The prompt states that "delete" is the replacement only where the named rule says to cut or delete the passage, and that where the rule says to rewrite, the replacement is the rewritten text, in a comment too. It names no list of such rules. Covers manuscript 1, 119, 120 and grant 37.
5. **Every finding has a place, a quoted passage and a replacement in text.** The prompt's "A finding" states:
   - every place the finding names, the first and every further one, is in the place form the prompt gives, a file of a `.tex` draft by its path from the main file's folder, and no place is named by a description such as "the table captions" (failures: manuscript 2, 24, 64, 65, 75);
   - the agent checks each place before it reports it: the quoted passage stands at that line, or in that paragraph of the command's output (failure: grant 51);
   - a finding that adds text (an absence, a missing citation, a missing explanation) quotes the sentence at the place where the added text belongs, and its replacement is that sentence, as it reads after every other finding on it, followed by the added text, the author's fact in square brackets (failures: manuscript 27 and grant 24);
   - a finding on a paragraph, a sentence list or a line break quotes the passage it changes and gives the changed text, in the forms of "Decisions taken in this brief" 1 (failures: manuscript 95 to 110, 117, 118 and grant 54 to 61);
   - a finding on a term used under several names gives each place with its passage and replacement, and a finding on tense gives each sentence with its replacement (failures: manuscript 36, 37, 39 to 42, 62 to 65);
   - a replacement is text that stands in place of the passage, "delete" as kind 4 says, or text with a bracketed fact; never an instruction such as "the past tense throughout", "Split before ...", "join lines", "keep it in one place only", "name the approaches with their citations", "add [citations]" or "a list introduced by ..." (failures: manuscript 2's second place, 13, 75, 78, 80, 112 and grant 13, 35, 38, 60, 61);
   - a count the finding states (sentences in a paragraph, words in a sentence) is counted, and a split leaves each part within the rule it was reported under (failures: manuscript 108 and grant 54).
6. **Two findings never conflict.** The prompt states that findings on one passage, or on two places of one claim, propose changes that can all be applied together, and that a passage that breaks two rules gets one replacement that ends both breaks. Covers grant 37 and 38 against grant 35 and 17.
7. **The line under each group says why the group stands where it does.** The prompt's "How you group them" states that every group heading is followed by one line, and that the line names what the reader loses or misreads through the group's breaks, which places the group in the order; a line that only restates the rule or describes the breaks does not. Failures: the eight groups with no line and the nine whose line does not say why, listed in `2-report.md`, condition 2.
8. **The review agent reads with the runner's file-reading tool, and runs one command only.** The prompt's "What you read" and "What you run" state that each file of a `.tex`, `.md` or `.txt` draft is read whole with the runner's file-reading tool, a long file in as many reads as it needs with nothing left out; that the one command the agent runs on such a file is `LC_ALL=C grep -n '[^ -~]' <file>`, for the prose standard's ASCII rule, as ruling D1 names it; that for a `.docx` it runs the command the prompt gives, and that same grep on that command's output through a pipe; and that no output is redirected to a file. The old sentence "The one command you run for the draft is the one the prompt gives for a `.docx`" did not say how a text draft is read, and the agent read it through a shell; the new text names the act to do instead. Failure: the manuscript review agent's six shell commands and its redirect to `rev_part3.txt` (`2-report.md`, condition 3).
9. **The session writes nothing during a run.** `SKILL.md` Steps 5 states that the session writes the prompt and the paths in the agent's start call itself, and Steps 3 that the file list is held in the session's message, not in a file. The old sentence "The session writes no file and adds no finding of its own" stood in Steps 7, after the run; the session wrote its files at Steps 3 and 5 to build the prompt, which the new text places at the step where the write happened. Failure: the session's four scratchpad files (`2-report.md`, "Deviations from the brief", bullet 1).

## What it must do

As "What to build" says, kind by kind. The prompt stays one block the session gives whole; each new sentence in it is a rule the agent can follow by reading. No sentence names a draft, a project, a person, a finding number or this plan, since the skill's "Rules" say everything specific to a repository comes from `.agents/plan.yaml`. The examples a rule gives are general, such as "this variation" for a determiner with its noun.

## Conventions

- The rules file's character set: ASCII, with no em or en dash and no spaced hyphen as an aside.
- One paragraph or bullet per source line, no hard wrapping.
- Plain prose: state the rule and stop; no history, no "previously", no reason longer than one clause.
- A rule is written once (`docs/dev/skill-layout.md`, "Where a rule goes"); a rule of the prose standard is named by its section, never restated.
