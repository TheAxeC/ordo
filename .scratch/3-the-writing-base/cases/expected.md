# Expected results of the known cases

Every passage of a draft that a table does not list is expected to be reported under no rule.

The expected results are read against the reports of step 3 and are not changed after its second run.

How the tables read:

- Each section below is one draft, and each row is one case.
- The rule is named as `/writing` names it: the prose standard by its section and the bold label of the bullet or, for a bullet or paragraph without one, its first words; `references/rules.md` by its group and heading; `references/registers.md` by its register and heading.
- The `.tex` place is `main.tex:<line>` or `sections.tex:<line>`, the line of the passage in that file. The `.docx` place is `paragraph <n>`, the number `docx_paragraphs.py` prints for the paragraph that holds the passage.
- A passage that breaks more than one rule stands once, in the first group in the order of the review prompt (the prose standard, then `references/rules.md`, then `references/registers.md`, and within a page in the order the rules stand), and the finding names each of the other rules it breaks, each on a line of its own and in that order. A passage that breaks a rule has one row, which gives the group it stands in and each other rule it names.
- A "nothing reported" row is expected for the quoted passage only; the rest of its paragraph is listed or unlisted on its own account.
- A rule judged on a whole draft has its count in its break row. The word count is the number of words `python3 skills/writing/templates/docx_paragraphs.py <draft>.docx` prints, without the paragraph numbers (`sed 's/^[0-9]*: //' | wc -w`). A report that places such a break anywhere in the paragraph or paragraphs named in its row is at its place.
- A rule judged on a section has the section's heading line as its place, and a report that places it at any line of that section is at its place.
- A break that has no passage because the rule asks for something the draft leaves out is placed at the heading of the section where the rule asks for it, and the quoted passage is that heading.
- A list in a `.docx` prints one paragraph for its introduction and one for each item, the item as a space, its number, a space and its text. In the `.tex` form a list is an `enumerate` environment, one item to a source line.

## Rules with no case

- "Hedging / Hedge strength": it states the scale of hedging devices and asks nothing of a passage.
- "Flagged terms / Alternatives": its text says no finding is reported under it.
- "Flagged terms / Discipline exceptions", the exceptions "paradigm shift", "robust estimator" and "navigate": they name the philosophy of science, statistics and wayfinding research, and no draft is in those disciplines. The exception "landscape" has its clean case in `sciences/`.
- "F. Emphasis and formatting / code fences carry a language tag" and "one blank line ... around code fences": neither a `.tex` nor a `.docx` draft holds a code fence. The sub-rule of the same bullet that asks for one paragraph or bullet per source line has its cases, and the sub-rule that asks for one blank line before headings holds in every draft without a break row of its own.
