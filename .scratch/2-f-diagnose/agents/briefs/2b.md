## Decisions taken in this brief (each reversible, none silent)

1. The form takes no argument after `premise`, as the ruling writes it. A step's text with several parts that ask for a cause gets one diagnosis per part, in order, in the one record.
2. The symptom of the form is what the step's text says happens. The step's text is what `spec` "What it reads" 4 reads: the step's line, the rulings its tags name and its Step 0. A part counts by what it asks, in whatever words.
3. The diagnosis writes its result in the step's Step 0, as the red-line form does, so the form works run by hand before `/spec` and run by `/spec` itself, and a second `/spec` run finds the cause there.
4. The red command is carried into the brief as a check, and the test as a case where one was written, since a defect in text has no test.
5. The fix is made by the step's builder from the brief, never on main by the diagnosis.
6. A step's text with no such part is a refusal with a Stops row of its own, "No part to investigate".
7. A cause not found stops a step whose only item it is, since `/spec` would otherwise write a brief with nothing to build.
8. A red command that is green on main is a false premise and goes to `spec` Steps 2.
9. `spec` Steps 4 says "changes no file of the checkout outside the ledger" in place of "read-only", since the diagnosis writes its record and the step's Step 0.
10. The glossary entry **Step 0** names the cause a step's text asks for beside a red line's. The entry **premise** is not changed, and no term is added: the step's line touches the glossary where it names the forms, and no entry does.
11. The row of `plan-orchestration`'s "Use instead" joins the step's paths, since it lists the forms and would leave one out.
12. The description's sentence on the hand-over is reworded, since the form hands its fix to no builder's round. `README.md` and the other places the premises name stay unchanged: none lists the forms.
13. No version number changes, as in steps 1, 2 and 2a of this plan.
14. The texts say "part" for what a step's text asks, and keep "item" for an item of a brief's "What to build", as `spec` Steps 4 uses it.
