## Decisions taken in this brief (each reversible, none silent)

1. The words that set a plan aside are named ("set aside", "thrown out", "stopped", "the entry is redone"), and the reading decides, since the ruling says "judged by reading" and K1's real rulings use "stops", "thrown out" and "redone".
2. A ruling that says the entry is redone reaches only the plans that stood when it was given, told by the dates when they differ and by the ruling's naming of the plan when they are equal, so the redo plan that ruling ordered is never set aside by it (K5), and K1's same-date pair is still decided.
3. A set-aside plan's bullets are no carried rulings for any entry, since the plan is thrown out whole; this covers plan 3's Question 2 on entries 4 and 5.
4. A ruling may set aside part of a plan (K7), since the ruling keeps the user's rulings in force until the user ends them, and the user can end some of them.
5. A reversed set-aside ruling sets nothing aside (K6), and a carried copy of a set-aside bullet settles nothing (K8), both for the same reason.
6. The set-aside rulings are shown in round 1 with the plans they set aside (item 2), so the user sees why the old plan's decisions are asked again; a set-aside ruling is a carried ruling only when it also names the entry and stands outside a set-aside plan, as the term says.
7. The minor version rises, since what `grill` asks changes.
