# Brief: 12c, steps by part, a brief that states a part, new work sorted by its reason, and the pin over every Claude config folder

Read `docs/dev/change-standard.md` first; its rules govern this step unchanged. Then, in full: `docs/dev/skill-layout.md`, `skills/repo-setup/templates/docs/dev/prose-standard.md`, `docs/glossary.md`, and the ADR this brief names under "What is on the tree".

A design ruling decides what is built. It never exempts the code: every line is written to the standards pages, so that people can read, use and maintain it.

## Why this step exists

The plan skills cut a roadmap entry into too many steps. Three rules do it:

- `plan` drafts the step list "from the gate, one step per verifiable piece of it", so the gate's checks become steps and the build is cut along them.
- `spec` requires a brief to dictate the content of every change, so a step must be small enough to dictate, which splits "build X" from "wire X in" and cuts one feature into pieces.
- `plan-orchestration` sends every finding that "changes the scope" to the user as a stop. With a step's scope being its dictated text, almost every finding qualifies, and comes back as a lettered step.

The ruling "Steps by part" (`plan.md`, Rulings) replaces these with one rule: a plan's steps are the parts of its entry, a brief states a part, and new work goes where its reason sends it. New steps and new roadmap entries stay possible; what changes is the reason each is created for. This step writes that rule into the skills, and with it three smaller changes the user ruled into the same step.

## The ruling this step builds, as the user agreed it

From `plan.md`, Rulings, "Steps by part (2026-10-04)":

- A plan's step list is drafted from the entry's goal as its parts.
- A step is a part that must exist and work before another part is built on it, or a point where the user reads or decides before the rest goes on.
- A gate's check runs inside the step that delivers what it checks.
- Wiring in, terms and documentation belong to the step that builds the thing.
- Bookkeeping is done in a commit the orchestrator already makes, never as a step.
- New work goes by its reason:
  - a finding inside the step's part is fixed in its repair rounds, whatever files it reaches;
  - a ruling on a part not yet built changes that step's brief;
  - a part the entry needs that no step builds, or a landed part found wrong or short, is a new step, by the user's ruling or a self-rule choice;
  - work outside the entry's goal is a new roadmap entry through `/roadmap add`;
  - a finding that changes a public shape or an established decision is an open item.

How "built on it" is read (this brief's reading, Decision 1): a later part is built on an earlier one when the later part needs the earlier one's result, not only its existence: a landed or tagged text, or a run or a decision that only the user, or a session other than the step's builder, can make on the landed result. Two parts whose content is known in advance are one step even when one uses the other (a script and the setup text that copies it). A run the builder itself makes, read by the user, is part of the step's check, and the user's reading blocks nothing after it. No number of steps is a rule, a target or a limit anywhere in the text: the number follows from the parts.
