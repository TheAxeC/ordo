## What to build

The step rests on two rulings of the user. Each requirement below serves one of them. Word the pages yourself; nothing here is dictated text.

Ruling "The budget is a ceiling": a report that finds exactly the budget's number of major points is bad, since it reads as a review that always finds that number; the number of major points comes from the paper, and the budget only cuts.

Ruling "What a second round is": a second round is mainly about what improved and changed against round 1: how the authors responded to each of the referee's comments and whether that is satisfactory; separately, whether the revision introduced another extremely major defect, which is exceptional; a second-round report whose points are mostly other items than those of round 1 is wrong.

### The count of points (both rounds)

In `agents/referee_agent.md`, "The selection", with `templates/selection_template.md` and the places of `SKILL.md` that name the budget:

1. The page orders the referee's work so that the budget comes last: it lists the comments, merges them, grades the points and writes down how many major and how many minor points it found, and only then takes up the budget. The grading step says the budget plays no part in a grade. The found counts stand in `selection.md`, in a place the template gives them, beside the budget, so the user sees at the selection stop how many the paper has and how many the budget cut.
2. A point is major only when a main claim of the paper fails, or cannot be judged at all, unless the point is settled. A weakness that bears on a main claim and leaves it standing is minor: wording, reporting, a missing detail, a further comparison that would strengthen a claim the evidence already carries. A missing comparison is major only when the claim is that comparison. A point the referee is unsure about is minor.
3. The budget is a ceiling and never a number to reach. The page says what to do in place of each way of reaching it: a point keeps the grade item 2 gives it, one weakness stays one point, and room left under the budget stays empty. A selection with fewer points than the budget, or with no major point, is a complete selection.
4. One point is one weakness. Two comments are about the same weakness when they are about the same claim or passage and the same action of the authors would settle both. Comments about different weaknesses are never merged into one point; a comment that belongs to no selected point is left out with its reason.
5. When the paper has more major points or more minor points than the budget, the lowest-ranked points are cut. A cut point stays a point: the template gives the points over the budget a place of their own, each with its grade, its passage and its merged comments, and a reason that names the budget. The user sees them at the selection stop and may move one in. The count line still adds up.
6. The length of the budget is a ceiling in the same way: the report is as long as its points need, and "The report" says so.
7. `SKILL.md` says the same where it speaks of the budget (the intake's question, the Done line of the selection, the anti-pattern row, the description where it needs it, within its 1,024 characters), by naming the referee's section and not by repeating it.

### The second round

In `references/second_round.md`, with the second-round sentences of `agents/referee_agent.md`, both templates and `SKILL.md`:

8. The page states what a second round is for: how the authors answered each first-round point and whether the answer is enough. The selection and the report of a second round are built on the first-round points. A remaining first-round point holds only what the first-round point asked and what of it is still missing; nothing else is merged into it and it is not retitled to hold more.
9. The EIC's check gives each first-round point its status, what the revision and the response did for it and, where the status is not "resolved", what is still missing. This part exists on the tree; keep it.
10. A new point is an exception. One is raised only when both hold: the revision introduced the defect (it stands in material the revision added or changed, or the revision made a first-round point worse to that degree), and the defect by itself makes a main claim of the revised paper fail or impossible to judge. Each new point names what the revision added or changed. The page says that most second rounds have no new point.
11. Since the panel's page "New Issue Detection" asks the EIC for more than that, item 4 of `references/second_round.md` gives the EIC the test of item 10 in the session's own words: the EIC writes as a new issue only what passes the test, and writes everything else it notices under a separate heading of `revision-check.md` for other observations.
12. Every other observation is listed among the left-out comments with a reason and is never a point and never merged into a first-round point: the EIC's other observations, a weakness that stood in the first version which the first-round comments did not raise, and the comments of the five reviewers, when the user asked for them, that are neither about a first-round point's remainder nor pass the test of item 10.
13. The referee does not grade a remaining first-round point above its first-round grade; the user may still regrade at the selection stop. A first-round point resolved in part whose remainder is small becomes a minor point or is left out with its reason. Where the first-round points are read from the response letter and carry no grade, each is graded by item 2 on what it asked.
14. The recommendation of a second round rests on the statuses of the first-round points, the major ones first, and on whether a new point stands. When every first-round major point is resolved, or what remains of it is a minor point, and no new point stands, the recommendation is accept or minor revision. The page says this and leaves the value otherwise to the referee's judgment.
15. The report of a second round keeps the journal's fields. Its general comments open with what the revision changed and which first-round points it resolved. Under the major and the minor points stand the remaining first-round points, each naming its first-round number and what is still missing, then any new point, marked as new. `agents/referee_agent.md`, "The report", and `templates/referee_report_template.md` show this form for a second round beside the first-round form, and `templates/selection_template.md` carries what items 8 to 13 need (the first-round number of a point, what the revision changed for a new point, the place for points over the budget).
16. `SKILL.md` stays the caller: change it only where its own words would now be false or where the stop "The selection" must show something new, such as the points over the budget. Its `metadata.version` stays "1.0.0": the skill is new in this plan, and a plan raises a skill's version once. Reread each sentence of the five files that describes a file or a round as a whole, and change the ones the step makes false.

Keep everything else of the skill as it is: the calls, the intake's questions and defaults (7 major points, 7 minor points, 1,200 words), the stops, the run folder, the file names, the roles started and how. No label of the skill's figure changes; if a change you need would make a label of `docs/figures/gen_figures.py`, function `referee()`, false, stop and report it.

## Cases

Each is checked by reading the pages as a referee agent would follow them.

- C1. The reports hold three weaknesses under which a main claim fails and ten that bear on the main claims and leave them standing; the budget is 7 major points. Expected: three major points, the found count says three major, and the ten are minor points, cut points or left-out comments.
- C2. The reports hold ten weaknesses under which a main claim fails; the budget is 7. Expected: seven selected, three shown as major points over the budget with a reason that names the budget, and the found count says ten.
- C3. The reports hold no weakness under which a main claim fails. Expected: a selection with no major point is complete, and the report is written.
- C4. Twelve comments of four reviewers are about four different passages, each settled by a different action of the authors. Expected: four points or fewer with the rest left out; never one point that holds all twelve.
- C5. The selected points need about 500 words; the budget is 1,200 words. Expected: a report of about 500 words.
- C6. Second round: every first-round major point is resolved, and the EIC notices four further weaknesses, none in material the revision changed. Expected: no new point, the four among the left-out comments, and a recommendation of accept or minor revision.
- C7. Second round: the revision replaced the method, and the new method has a defect under which the main claim fails. Expected: one new point, marked as new, naming what the revision changed.
- C8. Second round: a first-round minor point got no answer in the response letter. Expected: it stays a minor point that names its first-round number, or is left out with a reason; the referee does not make it a major point.
- C9. Second round: a first-round major point is resolved in part, and what is missing is one sentence of wording. Expected: a minor point or a left-out comment, not a major point.
- C10. Second round: a first-round major point on the balance of the comparisons is resolved in part, and the EIC notices that the new abstract overstates a result the revision added, which does not make a main claim fail. Expected: the first-round point holds only what it asked and what of that is missing; the new observation is a left-out comment and is not merged into the point.
- C11. Second round: two first-round major points are not resolved, and what is missing in each still makes a main claim fail. Expected: two major points that name their first-round numbers, and a recommendation the referee judges, which may be major revision.

The builder's first task, before any change, is the first read of every case above on the unchanged tree, with each case's result noted. When the first read finds a case the brief's own rules get wrong, the builder stops there, before changing anything, and hands back the first read and that case, with the rule and the result it gives. The orchestrator rules on the case and resumes the builder with the ruling, and the final report carries it.
