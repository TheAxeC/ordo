# Literature strategist: the synthesis

The literature strategist agent reads this page at a start that writes the synthesis, section 7 of `review.md`.

## The synthesis

- The synthesis is organised by the parts of the research question: one section for each part, P1 to Pn, headed with its number and its sentence as `config.md` records them, with the themes of the matrix that answer it under it as paragraph groups.
- Each paragraph group compares the results of the sources it cites: where they agree, where they differ, and why where the sources say why.
- A comparison sets only like measures against each other: an F1 against an F1, an accuracy against an accuracy, a count against a count of the same thing. A result in another measure is stated beside it as its own result, with no comparison drawn.
- Two results that conflict on the same question, under the same assumptions, are a divergent finding. Two results under different assumptions are stated as results under those assumptions, each with its assumption, and never as a debate or a divergent finding.
- Each section of a part ends with a sentence that names the searches that found nothing for that part, by their numbers in the search record with their database and count, or says that every search for the part found sources.
- Each claim cites its sources as `[@key]`, with the key of the source's entry in `refs.bib`.
- Each citation rests on the annotation of a source read in the run. Before it writes a cited sentence, the agent checks that the source's annotation or saved text holds what the sentence attributes to it.
- A survey is cited as a survey: a study the survey reports is cited by its own entry when the agent has read it, and otherwise the sentence says that the survey reports the study, as in "a survey reports one study of irrigation scheduling [@key]".
- The synthesis claims only what an annotation supports.
- A sentence may name what the absence of a work listed under "No entry written" leaves open, as the gaps and the limitations do.
- In lit-review mode the synthesis has the structure of a review paper.
  - It opens with the topic and its rationale, and the scope and boundaries: what is included and excluded, the period, the disciplines and the geographies. The geographies are those of the geography dimension of the distribution advisory, or the scope says that the included sources state none.
  - It then gives the review methodology (the search, the criteria and the number of sources) and a preview of the parts of the question and their themes.
  - It then has the section of each part, with a summary of what the literature says on that part before its sentence on the searches that found nothing.
  - It continues with the cross-cutting synthesis: the convergent findings, the divergent findings as the bullet above defines them, or one sentence that says the sources hold none, and the methods that dominate or are underused.
  - It ends with the gaps of section 6, each with the parts it concerns, a proposed research agenda, the key takeaways and the implications for practice or policy.
