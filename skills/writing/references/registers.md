# Registers

A register sets the voice and the terminology the reader of a field expects. The register of a draft is the one of its discipline. Four registers are kept: the Sciences (Natural, Applied), the Social Sciences, Engineering and CS, and Medicine and Health. A draft in another discipline has no register rule here.

Where a register line and its voice rule differ on the pronoun, the voice rule holds. A venue's author guidelines hold over a voice rule when the project names the venue. A finding names a rule by its register and its heading, as in "Sciences (Natural, Applied) / Voice".

## Sciences (Natural, Applied)

### Register

The text is formal, impersonal and focused on the method.

### Terminology

Measurements are precise and in SI units, and statistics use statistical notation. Example: "The sample was heated to 350 degrees Celsius for 2 hours, yielding a conversion rate of 87.3% (SD = 2.1)."

### Voice

A procedure takes the passive, since its actor is irrelevant. The authors' decisions, contributions and results take "we". The authors never refer to themselves in the third person.

### What the reader looks for

The reader looks for precision of measurement and method. A methods or results section gives it.

## Social Sciences

### Register

The text is formal, informed by theory and aware of its participants.

### Terminology

Theoretical constructs are named and variables are operationalized. Example: "We employed semi-structured interviews to explore how participants understood institutional change (N = 24)."

### Voice

The researcher's decisions take "we". A procedure takes the passive where the participants or the instrument matter. The authors never refer to themselves in the third person.

### What the reader looks for

The reader looks for transparency about limitations. A methods or results section gives it.

## Engineering and CS

### Register

The text is formal, oriented to the problem and its solution, and precise about specifications.

### Terminology

Technical specifications and performance metrics are stated. Example: "The proposed algorithm achieves O(n log n) complexity, outperforming the baseline by 34% on the benchmark dataset."

### Voice

A procedure takes the passive, since its actor is irrelevant. The authors' decisions, contributions and results take "we". The authors never refer to themselves in the third person.

### What the reader looks for

The reader looks for reproducibility of results. A methods or results section gives it.

## Medicine and Health

### Register

The text is formal, conscious of the evidence hierarchy and clinically precise.

### Terminology

Clinical terms, diagnostic criteria and statistical reporting are used as the field defines them. Example: "Patients receiving the intervention showed a 40% reduction in readmission rates (RR = 0.60, 95% CI [0.45, 0.80], p = .001)."

### Voice

A procedure takes the passive, since its actor is irrelevant. The authors' decisions, contributions and results take "we". The authors never refer to themselves in the third person.

### What the reader looks for

The reader looks for adherence to reporting guidelines. A methods or results section names the reporting guideline the paper follows. Whether the paper meets that guideline is a check of content that this review does not make.
