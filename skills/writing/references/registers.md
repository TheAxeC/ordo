# Registers

A draft is held to the register of its discipline. A draft in the humanities or in education is held to the prose standard and to `references/rules.md` only. So is a draft that fits none of the four registers below.

## Voice

The prose standard's actor test decides where the passive is used: where the actor is irrelevant.

- **The Sciences, Engineering and CS, and Medicine and Health.** A procedure takes the passive. The authors' decisions, contributions and findings take "we".
  - "The data were normalised." stays passive.
  - "The authors chose a learning rate of 0.01." is reported, and the replacement is "We chose a learning rate of 0.01."
- **The Social Sciences.** The researcher's decisions take "we". A procedure takes the passive where the participants or the instrument matter.
- The authors are never referred to in the third person.
- The replacement of a third-person reference to the authors is "we", also for a draft with one author, and never "I" or "my".
- A venue's author guidelines, when the project names the venue, hold over these rules.

## Sciences (Natural, Applied)

- **Register**: Formal, impersonal, method-focused.
- **Terminology**: Precise measurements, SI units, statistical notation.
- **Voice**: As "Voice" gives for the Sciences, Engineering and CS, and Medicine and Health.
- **The reader looks for**: Precision of measurement and method.
- **Example**: "The sample was heated to 350 degrees C for 2 hours, yielding a conversion rate of 87.3% (SD = 2.1)."

## Engineering and CS

- **Register**: Formal, problem-solution oriented, specification-precise.
- **Terminology**: Technical specifications, performance metrics.
- **Voice**: As "Voice" gives for the Sciences, Engineering and CS, and Medicine and Health.
- **The reader looks for**: Reproducibility of results.
- **Example**: "The proposed algorithm achieves O(n log n) complexity, outperforming the baseline by 34% on the benchmark dataset."

## Medicine and Health

- **Register**: Formal, evidence-hierarchy conscious, clinical precision.
- **Terminology**: Clinical terms, diagnostic criteria, statistical reporting.
- **Voice**: As "Voice" gives for the Sciences, Engineering and CS, and Medicine and Health.
- **The reader looks for**: Adherence to reporting guidelines.
- **Example**: "Patients receiving the intervention showed a 40% reduction in readmission rates (RR = 0.60, 95% CI [0.45, 0.80], p = .001)."

## Social Sciences

- **Register**: Formal, theory-informed, participant-aware.
- **Terminology**: Theoretical constructs, operationalized variables.
- **Voice**: As "Voice" gives for the Social Sciences.
- **The reader looks for**: Transparency about limitations.
- **Example**: "We employed semi-structured interviews to explore how participants understood institutional change (N = 24)."

## What the reader looks for

- A methods or results section that leaves out what its register's reader looks for is reported. The replacement adds it, or names the fact the author must supply.
- In Medicine and Health, a methods section that names no reporting guideline is reported.
- Whether a medical paper follows its reporting guideline is a check of content that the review does not make.
