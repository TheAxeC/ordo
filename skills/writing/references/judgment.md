# Writing judgment

Some choices in a draft are settled only by judgment, since no word list or script can decide them. The `writing` skill and each writing skill built on it use these judgments when they read a draft.

## Clarity test

For each paragraph, ask whether the text still makes sense without it. The answer decides what happens to the paragraph:

| Answer | Paragraph | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Yes, and nothing is lost | Not needed | Delete it |
| Yes, but context is lost | Supporting | Keep it, and keep it short |
| No, the argument breaks | Load-bearing | Give it the most careful writing |

A load-bearing paragraph gets several drafts and a supporting paragraph gets one. A text that gives every paragraph equal weight fails the test.

## The reader's four questions

The text guides its reader, and at any point the reader can answer four questions:

1. **Where am I?** The section structure and its signposts.
2. **Why am I here?** The connection to the research question.
3. **What should I take away?** The point of the section.
4. **Where am I going next?** The logic of the transition.

A text where any of the four is unclear is revised, however accurate its content.

## Voice by discipline

| Discipline | Voice | What makes it credible |
|---|---|---|
| Hard sciences | Impersonal, passive, hedged | Precision of measurement and method |
| Social sciences | Semi-personal, active, qualified | Transparency about limitations |
| Humanities | Personal, argumentative, interpretive | Depth of engagement with sources |
| Engineering | Direct, oriented to the specification | Reproducibility of results |
| Medicine | Structured, focused on the protocol | Adherence to reporting guidelines |

A discipline's trusted passive voice is used where the actor is irrelevant, as prose standard, section E, allows.

A voice taken from another discipline is the wrong one. Examples are "We argue that" in a physics paper and "The data indicates" in a humanities essay.

## The "so what" of each section

| Section | The reader's question | The writer's job |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Why should I care about this topic? | Open with a real-world consequence or a gap in knowledge |
| Literature review | What is missing from what we know? | Build to the gap rather than only summarising the literature |
| Method | Can I trust these results? | Show rigour, and acknowledge limitations up front |
| Results | What did you find? | Lead with the finding, not the statistical test |
| Discussion | What does this mean for the field? | Connect back to the gap, and state the delta clearly |
| Conclusion | What should I remember? | One sentence that captures the contribution |
